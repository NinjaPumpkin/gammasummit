## Section 2: Same Ticker, Same Expiry

A print belongs to one contract. Compare it with **that ticker's** map, scoped to **that expiry** where you can. Then place the strike:

- Where is it relative to **spot**?
- Near a **floor**, a **ceiling**, the **King Node**, or in an **air pocket**?
- How large is the exposure there — **magnitude matters more than color**.

Two cautions: **a QQQ strike is not an NQ price** — any ETF-to-future mapping needs its own conversion, recorded with the time, never a fixed multiplier. And **keep each index separate** — a SPY print is read against SPY's map.

Write it in one line: *"SPY 590 call, 12 DTE, bought at the ask — strike at the King Node above spot."*

## Section 3: Flow Is One Reason the Map Reshuffles

The map is not static. When positioning changes, nodes grow, shrink or move — a **reshuffle**. New options positions are one cause: when traders open size at a strike, the other side — often a dealer — takes on exposure there.

But the map also changes when positions **close**, dealers **re-hedge**, **price moves**, and **time passes**. So heavy flow at a strike and a reshuffle there are **related evidence** — neither proves the other caused it.

Regime matters too: **positive gamma** tends to dampen moves, **negative gamma** tends to amplify them. Regime shapes **behavior**, not direction.

## Section 4: The Trinity, Side by Side

The Trinity: **SPX** (S&P 500 index options), **SPY** (the S&P 500 ETF) and **QQQ** (the Nasdaq-100 ETF). Read each on its own map and levels, then line them up:

| | Chart location | Map | Flow |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPX | | | |
| SPY | | | |
| QQQ | | | |

The trap: bullish flow on all three **feels** like three confirmations. It isn't. **SPX and SPY track the same index** — their agreement is close to **one** piece of evidence. **QQQ** is more independent, but many of the biggest stocks sit in both indices. More copies of similar flow add little; the chart and the map are the independent evidence.

## Section 5: Divergence Is a Warning; Finish With "Weakens If"

From the Heatseeker track:

- **3 of 3 aligned** — full confluence
- **2 of 3** — two agree and the third diverges or lags: reduced confidence; the bare minimum, at reduced size
- **1 of 3 or fewer** (total misalignment) — no trade

> Divergence is not opportunity. Divergence is a warning.

Don't pick the index that agrees with you. Reduce size or pass — and find out why they disagree.

Every combined read ends the same way: **chart · map · flow · read · weakens if** — with "weakens if" concrete: a level lost, a node that shrinks, open interest that falls back. When the chart gives no edge — a midpoint — flow can't create one.

## Section 6: Case Study Example

*Hypothetical — constructed for teaching.*

**SPY** has ranged between 578 and 590 for a week; price is at 581.40, near the **range low**. A large node at 578 acts as a **floor**; the **King Node** at 590 is the ceiling; positive gamma. The flow: the 590/600 bull call spread, with open interest rising the next day. Chart, map and flow agree — a move from the floor toward 590. (The spread runs to 600, but 590 is the structural target.)

Now the Trinity:

| | Chart | Map | Flow |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPX | Range low | Floor below | Bullish |
| SPY | Range low | Floor below | Bullish |
| QQQ | Range high | Ceiling above | Bearish |

SPX and SPY agree — close to one piece of evidence. **QQQ disagrees** at its ceiling. That's **2 of 3**: reduced size, or wait for QQQ to reject or break its ceiling.

**Weakens if:** SPY accepts below 578; the floor node shrinks or reshuffles lower; open interest at 590 falls back.

## Section 7: Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1 — Reading a print against the wrong board

A SPY print belongs on SPY's map. And an ETF strike is not a futures price.

---

### Mistake 2 — Counting SPX and SPY as two confirmations

They track the same index. Their agreement is expected.

---

### Mistake 3 — Ignoring the index that disagrees

Divergence is a warning. Reduce size or pass.
