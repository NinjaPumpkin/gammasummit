## Section 1: Chapter Objective

Tier 1 taught you to read one print by hand. On a busy day, thousands go by. Flowseeker gives you two ways to cope: **scores** that tell you where to look first, and **filters** that decide what you see at all.

Both help. Both can fool you. This chapter teaches four things:

1. What Flow Score and FlowBonus each tell you
2. Why scores sort attention — they aren't probabilities
3. How to build a readable feed: one tab, one question, one change at a time
4. Why a filtered view reads the way you filtered it — and what the summary bar actually covers

## Section 2: Two Scores, Two Questions

**Flow Score — which way?**

- Runs from **−100 to +100**
- Positive means classified **bullish**; negative, **bearish**; near zero, **unclear**
- It draws on the Tier 1 clues — side, sweeps, moneyness, DTE and premium — plus implied volatility

**How unusual? — premium rank and Vol/OI**

- The **Premium vs Ticker History** filter ranks a print's premium against that ticker's own trades over the last 20 days — a $1M print is routine on SPY and rare on a quiet name
- A high **Vol/OI** says today's activity is large relative to open interest
- Neither says anything about direction

*You may also see Skylit's **FlowBonus** ("how unusual") quoted by Flow AI. It isn't a column on the Live Feed — on the feed, premium rank and Vol/OI answer the same question.*

| Flow Score | How unusual (premium rank, Vol/OI) | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Strong (either way) | High | Directional **and** unusual — look first |
| Strong | Low | Clearly directional, but routine |
| Near zero | High | Unusual but unclear — look for a hedge or a spread |
| Near zero | Low | Lowest priority |

## Section 3: Scores Sort; They Aren't Probabilities

A Flow Score of **+80** does **not** mean an 80% chance of the stock going up. It means the print was classified strongly bullish.

A score is built from the same clues you learned in Tier 1, so it inherits their limits: side can't prove buying, a single leg can look like a different trade, and a print can be a close. The score can't see past those limits — but it can **sort** thousands of prints so the ones worth your time rise to the top.

Use scores like a search result: to decide what to read next. Then read the print, and weigh it against the chart and the map. When a score matters to your read, **write it down with the time you saw it.**

## Section 4: One Tab, One Question

Open **Filters** in the Live Feed and you get a drawer of filter groups — ticker, calls or puts, Flow Score, side, DTE, premium, open interest, moneyness and more. Your choices save to the **active tab**, so keep a tab per job:

| Tab | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| Broad watch | What is happening across the market? |
| Large prints | Where is the biggest money going today? |
| Ticker study | What is trading in the one name I care about? |
| Package study | Which big prints are parts of multi-leg trades? |

Build a tab **one change at a time** — premium floor, then universe, then sweeps, then score, then DTE and moneyness — and look after each change. Change four at once and you won't know which one emptied the feed or made it look bullish.

And **always keep one broad tab.** A narrow tab is a microscope: great for its question, terrible as your whole view of the market.

## Section 5: Your View Reads the Way You Filtered It

A filter decides what you **see**. It doesn't change what **happened**.

- **A premium floor isn't an institution filter.** A $500K minimum shows large prints — not funds.
- **A filtered feed reads the way you filtered it.** Filter to ask-side calls only and every row will look bullish, because you removed everything else.

The **summary bar** across the top — directional sentiment, net premium, FIR, calls and puts, put/call ratio and RVOL — is **market-wide**. It does not change with your tab's filters. So a narrow tab full of bullish rows can sit under a bearish bar, and neither one "wins": they measure different things. "Net premium +$40M" on the bar and "+$40M" you added up from one filtered ticker are very different statements.

So before any conclusion, ask two questions: **what is this measuring over?** and **would it still be true on my broad tab?**

## Section 6: Case Study Example

*Hypothetical — scores are illustrative, constructed for teaching.*

The SPY spread from Tier 1: 2,000 590 calls bought at the ask and 2,000 600 calls sold at the bid, in the same second.

**The scores:** the 590 calls get a strong positive Flow Score and rank near the top of SPY's premium history — they jump to the top of the feed. The 600 calls, sold at the bid, get a negative score. Two scores pointing opposite ways — but from Chapter 3 you know it's **one bullish spread**. The scores did their job: they got your attention. Reading it was still your job.

**The filters:** a second trader filters to ask-side calls only, sweeps only and a $1M floor — all at once. Their feed shows only bullish prints, and they conclude *"SPY is bullish."* But their tab could **only** show bullish-looking prints — and with every filter changed at once, they can't tell what shaped it. On the broad tab, SPY flow is mixed.

## Section 7: Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1 — Reading a score as a probability

+80 is a strong bullish classification, not an 80% chance. Scores sort; they don't forecast.

---

### Mistake 2 — Treating an unusually large premium as bullish

Premium rank and Vol/OI measure how unusual a print is, not which way it points.

---

### Mistake 3 — Reading your own filter as a market signal

A narrow, filtered tab reflects the filter. Check the broad tab — and remember the summary bar is market-wide, not your tab.

## Section 8: Key Takeaways

- Flow Score (−100 to +100) is direction; premium rank and Vol/OI are how unusual. A strong score on an unusually large print is where to look first.
- Scores sort attention; they are not probabilities. Write down a score with the time you saw it.
- One tab, one question. Change one filter at a time. Keep a broad tab.
- A premium floor shows large prints — not institutions.
- The summary bar is market-wide; your filtered rows are not. Ask what each number is measuring over.

***Remember:***

***A large print earns investigation. It does not identify a profitable trade.***
