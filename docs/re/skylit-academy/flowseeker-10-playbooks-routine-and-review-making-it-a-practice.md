## Course description

A playbook is a form for a hypothesis — location, map, flow, target, weakens if, decision — with flow never the trigger. The reward must be at least three times the risk, size is a separate decision, and no trade is always valid. Run the same simple day, save enough to review each decision (including the ones you skipped), and judge decisions, not outcomes.

## Section 1: Chapter Objective

You now have every piece: the chart, the map, flow read correctly, the tools, and the discipline for when things disagree. This last chapter turns them into a practice.

It teaches four things:

1. A playbook organises a hypothesis — it doesn't make the decision
2. Risk-to-reward and size are separate decisions, and no trade is valid

## Section 2: A Playbook Organises a Hypothesis

A playbook is a form with the same fields every time:

| Field | Answers |
|---|---|
| **Question** | What am I testing? |
| **Location** | Where is price in the chart structure? |
| **Map** | Which nodes matter, and what regime is it? |
| **Flow** | Does it support or challenge? |
| **Target** | The next structural level |
| **Weakens if** | The concrete things that would make me wrong |
| **Decision** | Trade, wait or pass — and at what size |

Flow sits fourth. **Flow is never the trigger.**

Two example playbooks, written as teaching templates from Skylit's charts-first and Heatseeker material:

- **Floor reversal** — price at a range low, a floor node beneath. Flow supports if bullish flow shows up at the level; it challenges if heavy put buying appears right at the floor. Target: the next ceiling. Weakens if price accepts below the floor or the floor node shrinks.
- **Ceiling rejection** — price at a range high, a ceiling node above. Flow supports if bullish flow fades or bearish flow appears; persistent call buying at the level is a conflict (Chapter 9). Target: the next floor. Weakens if price accepts above the ceiling or the ceiling node shrinks.

Both still have to pass Skylit's entry checklist before they become a trade: structure on the chart, Heatseeker confluence behind the direction, and at least 3-to-1 reward with a clean entry. (The summer bootcamp's stricter version adds a mapped liquidity sweep — all four gates, or no trade.)

## Section 3: Risk, Size and No Trade

**Risk-to-reward comes first.** The Skylit minimum is **3 to 1 reward to risk** (Glitch also writes it 1:3, risk to reward) — the distance to target at least three times the distance to invalidation. A great read at a bad entry is a bad trade.

**Size is its own decision.** Smaller size shrinks the dollars won or lost; it doesn't turn a bad setup good. Strong flow doesn't earn a bigger position by itself, and the Trinity rule still applies — 2 of 3 means reduced size.

**No trade is a valid outcome.** Pass when price is at a midpoint, the Trinity is totally misaligned (one diverging board still leaves 2 of 3, at reduced size), you can't write a concrete "weakens if", the risk-to-reward isn't there, or the evidence is an unresolved conflict.

## Section 4: A Simple, Repeatable Day

| When (New York time) | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Before the open** | Mark levels on the chart, read the maps, write your questions |
| **During the session** | Watch flow **at your planned levels**; fill in playbooks; recheck saved hypotheses when the map reshuffles |
| **After the close** | Contract totals in the Scanner — some data is still preliminary |
| **Next morning** | Check open interest on what you saved, using the actual OI date |
| **Weekly** | Review everything — wins, losses **and skipped trades** |

The routine's job is to stop you chasing: with levels and questions, flow has something to be evidence **for**.

For every setup you act on **or skip**, save: the contract and time, snapshots of the chart, map and feed, your filters, your hypothesis and the alternatives, what you knew **at that moment**, your "weakens if", and whether you traded it or only watched.

## Section 5: Review Honestly

- **Judge the decision, not the outcome.** A good decision can lose; a bad one can win. A winning trade taken at a midpoint was still a bad decision.
- **Count the misses, not just the wins.** Review only the winners and every pattern looks brilliant.
- **Keep the skipped trades.** They show whether your filter for passing is working.

The goal isn't to feel good about your trading. It's to find out what's true about it.

## Section 6: Case Study Example

*Hypothetical — constructed for teaching.*

**The playbook, filled in for SPY:** range 578–590, floor node at 578, King Node ceiling at 590, positive gamma; bull call spread flow with open interest growth — **supports**. Target 590. Weakens if price accepts below 578 — invalidation at 577.

**The decision:** at 581.40, reward to 590 is 8.60 and risk to 577 is 4.40 — about **2 to 1**, below the minimum. The trader **waits**. At 579, reward is 11 and risk is 2 — **5.5 to 1**. Now it qualifies.

**The week, reviewed:**

- **Monday** — that floor reversal at 579 hit its target. *Good decision, good outcome.*
- **Tuesday** — bought calls off a mid-range QQQ sweep without checking the map. It worked. *Bad decision, good outcome.*
- **Wednesday** — waited on an unresolved conflict at the 590 ceiling; price broke out. *Good decision, missed move.*
- **Thursday** — a complete floor reversal stopped out on acceptance below the floor. *Good decision, bad outcome.*

One mistake — and it was Tuesday's win.

## Section 7: Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1 — Treating a playbook as a trigger

A playbook is a checklist for a hypothesis. It doesn't fire trades on its own.

---

### Mistake 2 — Taking a good read at a bad entry

If the reward isn't at least three times the risk, wait or pass.

---

### Mistake 3 — Grading trades by profit

A win at a midpoint was still a bad decision; a clean stop-out on a complete playbook was a good one.

## Section 8: Key Takeaways

- A playbook is a form: question, location, map, flow, target, weakens if, decision. Flow is never the trigger.
- A trade needs chart structure, Heatseeker confluence and at least 3-to-1 reward with a clean entry — the bootcamp adds a mapped sweep as a fourth gate.
- Minimum risk-to-reward is 3 to 1. Size is separate. No trade is valid.
- Run the same day; save enough to review trades **and** skips.
- Judge decisions, not outcomes — and count the misses.

***Remember:***

***A large print earns investigation. It does not identify a profitable trade.***
