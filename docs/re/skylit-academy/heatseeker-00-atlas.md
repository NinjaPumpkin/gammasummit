## Section 2: Atlas: Bringing Confluence Onto the Chart

* * *

### **Atlas: Bringing Confluence Onto the Chart**

Atlas takes the key tools from Skylit Terminal and displays them directly on your chart in a cleaner, more actionable way.

Instead of switching between multiple screens to compare Heatseeker, Dark Pool levels, FlowSeeker, and price action, Atlas brings those data points together in one place.

This allows traders to quickly see the bigger picture when it matters most.

### Why Atlas Matters

The strongest trade ideas usually come from confluence.

Confluence means multiple independent signals are lining up around the same level.

For example, a level becomes more important when price is approaching:

-   A major Heatseeker King Node
    
-   A Dark Pool level
    
-   FlowSeeker activity
    
-   A key technical level on the chart
    

When several of these line up together, that area may become a higher-probability reaction zone.

### Example: King Node + Dark Pool Level

If price is pulling into a major King Node while a Dark Pool level is sitting in the same area, that level deserves attention.

The King Node tells us dealer positioning is meaningful there.

The Dark Pool level tells us large hidden activity may also be present there.

When price reacts from that area, the trader is not relying on one signal alone. They are seeing multiple layers of market structure support the same idea.

### Deflections and Rejections

Atlas helps traders spot areas where price may:

-   Bounce from support
    
-   Reject from resistance
    
-   Stall at a major level
    
-   Deflect away from a dealer positioning zone
    

The goal is not to blindly trade every node or Dark Pool level.

The goal is to identify where multiple pieces of evidence come together.

### Simple Summary

-   Atlas makes confluence easier to see.
    
-   Heatseeker shows where dealer positioning exists.
    
-   Dark Pool levels show where large hidden activity may be present.
    
-   FlowSeeker shows where options activity is coming in.
    
-   Price action shows how the market is reacting in real time.
    

**When these tools align, traders gain a clearer view of where the strongest reaction zones may be.**

**Confluence is key, and Atlas puts that confluence directly on the chart in a way that is easier to understand and act on.**

## Section 3: Understanding Expirations in Atlas

* * *

### **Understanding Expirations in Atlas**

### In Heatseeker, gamma exposure is organized by expiration.

Each column on the heatmap represents a different contract expiration. For example, one column may show today’s expiration, while another may show next week, two weeks out, three weeks out, and so on.

-   Atlas allows you to bring that same expiration logic directly onto your chart.
    
-   Using the **Expiration** dropdown, traders can choose how many expirations they want reflected on the chart.

## Section 4: Why This Matters

### **Why This Matters**

A major node may exist several expirations out, but that same strike may not be important for the current expiration, like in the case of NVDA.

-   The king node is centered near spot price on the current column.

## Section 5: For example:

### **For example:**

NVDA may have a large king node at **185** three weeks out, but the current expiration may only show a small node at that same level.

-   That means Atlas can look very different depending on which expiration setting you choose.
    

* * *

### How Traders Should Use It

Your expiration setting should match your trading style.

-   For **0DTE scalpers**, the current expiration is usually the most important because dealers are actively hedging today’s contracts. These traders should generally focus on **Current**.
    
-   For **swing traders**, further expirations may matter more because larger positioning can build several days or weeks out. These traders may want to include additional expirations to see where larger market structure is developing.
    

* * *

### Simple Rule

Do not assume every king node matters equally to every trade.

A king node three weeks out may matter for a swing trade, but it may not be the most relevant level for a same-day scalp.

The goal is to match Atlas to your timeframe:

-   **Scalping / 0DTE:** Focus on current expiration.
    
-   **Intraday trading:** Start with current, then compare nearby expirations if needed.
    
-   **Swing trading:** Include further expirations to identify larger positioning.
    

Changing the expiration setting does not change the data. It changes which expiration data is being displayed on your chart.

### Orbs (Classic)

-   Classic Orbs are much simpler in design compared to Orbs V2.
    
    -   More intense, brighter colors tell us that a node is higher in value.
        
    -   Less intense, dull colors tell us that a node is smaller in value.
        
-   You can adjust "Opacity" - this will adjust the max brightness / intensity.
    
-   Adjusting orb size will dictate how big or small you want your orbs to be.

## Section 7: Orbs V2

* * *

### **Orbs V2**

Orbs V2 allows you to customize how Heatseeker nodes appear on your chart.

Orb Size

Controls the overall size of the orbs.

-   Higher values = larger, more prominent zones.
    
-   Lower values = smaller, cleaner appearance.
    
-   Recommended if you want important levels to stand out more.
    

* * *

Min Clamp

Sets the minimum size an orb can be.

-   Higher values make smaller nodes easier to see.
    
-   Lower values allow weak nodes to remain subtle.
    
-   Useful if you don't want minor levels disappearing entirely.
    

* * *

Max Clamp

Sets the maximum size an orb can reach.

-   Higher values make dominant nodes much larger.
    
-   Lower values prevent large nodes from overwhelming the chart.
    
-   Useful for balancing readability in volatile sessions.
    

* * *

Min Opacity

Controls how visible weaker nodes are.

-   Higher values make all nodes easier to see.
    
-   Lower values fade weaker nodes into the background.
    
-   Helps reduce chart clutter.
    

* * *

Max Opacity

Controls the visibility of major nodes.

-   Higher values make strong nodes stand out more.
    
-   Lower values create a more uniform appearance across all nodes.
    

* * *

King Opacity

Controls the visibility of the single strongest node on the chart (the King Node).

-   Higher values make the King Node immediately obvious.
    
-   Lower values make it blend more naturally with surrounding nodes.
    

* * *

Scroll as Replay

When enabled, Orbs V2 only uses information available at that point in time as you scroll backward through the chart.

This allows you to:

-   Study how dealer positioning developed throughout the session.
    
-   Review historical setups without future data influencing the visualization.
    
-   Create a more realistic replay experience.
    

Recommended: Enabled for replay and historical review.

## Section 8: Derived Orbs

* * *

## Derived Orbs Overlay

Some charts inside Atlas use **derived exposure**, meaning the chart is borrowing GEX/VEX levels from a related product.

-   **ES** borrows levels from **SPXW/SPY**
    
-   **NQ** borrows levels from **QQQ**
    
-   **SPY** levels can be seen on **SPXW**
    
-   **SPXW** levels can be seen on **SPY**
    

This allows traders to see important dealer positioning from the options market directly on futures or related instruments (SPXW to SPY).

Because ES, NQ, SPX, SPY, SPXW and QQQ do not always move perfectly together, the borrowed levels may shift slightly as the session develops.

That small movement is what we call the **wiggle**.

### Why the Wiggle Is Good

-   The wiggle does **not** mean the data is wrong.
    
-   It means the overlay is staying aligned with live price movement.
    
-   Instead of forcing borrowed levels to stay frozen in one place all day, Atlas adjusts them so the level remains accurate relative to the source product.
    
-   This helps make sure that when price tags a derived level on ES or NQ, it is also lining up with the equivalent level on SPXW/SPY or QQQ at that moment.
    

### What Traders Should Know

-   If derived levels move slightly minute to minute, that is expected.
    
-   It means Atlas is keeping the borrowed exposure levels synced with the live market.
    
-   If a level briefly disappears, it usually means the source data temporarily dropped. Rather than showing a level in the wrong place, Atlas hides it until the data is reliable again.
    

### Simple Summary

-   Derived levels are borrowed levels from related products.
    
-   The wiggle happens because Atlas is constantly adjusting those borrowed levels to match live price movement.

## Section 9: Setting up for 0DTE 

* * *

### **Setting up for 0DTE**

### When scalping indexes, settings matter.

We arent looking to take scalps based off a node that is 3 days out. Gamma becomes increasingly dominant as a contract approaches expiration. The **current** expiration column is what 0DTE traders should be most focused on.

-   When toggling your settings on Atlas, you should set your nodes to current if you are looking to scalp 0DTE.
    
-   The amount of nodes you want your Chart to express will be dependent on personal preference. Atlas will express the top # nodes on the map based on the number of your choosing. 3 to 5 nodes is typically recommended.

## Section 10: Setting up for Swing Trading

* * *

### **Setting up for Swing Trading**

### Setting up your chart looks much different when swing trading.

The relationship between GEX and VEX for swing traders:

1.  GEX is used for intraday entries
    
2.  VEX is used for bigger picture price targets.
    
    1.  It is typically good practice to trade when GEX and VEX are in confluence.
        

The main difference for swing traders is the amount of expirations to reflect on your Atlas chart. Scalpers use "current", whereas swing trader may use "current + next N".

-   The amount of expirations to show can be adjusted to your liking.
